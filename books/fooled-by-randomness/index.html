## Notes

The core idea: we judge decisions by outcomes, but outcomes
are mostly noise. The right way is to think in alternative
histories — a trader who got rich on one leveraged bet isn't
skilled, he's the survivor we happen to observe.

Survivorship bias is everywhere once you see it. Every
founder story I read now gets a mental asterisk: where are
the people who did exactly the same thing and blew up?

Checking numbers too often means consuming noise. The
shorter the window, the more randomness dominates the
signal. I moved my analytics review from daily to weekly
and lost nothing.

Taleb's tone gets exhausting, but the math underneath is
sound: rare events dominate outcomes, and we are wired to
underestimate them.
