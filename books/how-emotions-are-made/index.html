## Notes

This one quietly rewired how I think about people. Emotions
aren't hardwired reactions that happen to us — the brain
constructs them on the fly from bodily signals, context,
and the concepts we've learned. Anger isn't a circuit that
fires; it's a prediction.

The body-budget idea is the most practical part. The brain's
main job is regulating energy, and a lot of what we label
"emotion" is really a budget problem: bad sleep, no food,
too much cortisol. Before assuming a hard conversation is
about the conversation, check the budget.

Emotional granularity is a skill worth training. The finer
your vocabulary for what you're feeling, the more options
your brain has for responding. "Stressed" is a dead end;
"overcommitted and behind on one thing" is actionable.

For product work: users don't have fixed emotional
reactions to interfaces. Context and expectation construct
the experience, which means we have more design leverage
than we think.
