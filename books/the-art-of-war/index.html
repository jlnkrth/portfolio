## Notes

Barely a book about war. It's about positioning: doing the
work before the confrontation so the confrontation becomes
unnecessary. The best move is the fight that never happens.

The strategy ideas map cleanly onto product work. Winning
without fighting is basically Thiel's "escape competition"
two and a half thousand years early — don't attack a strong
position, go where the opponent isn't.

Know the terrain, know yourself, know the enemy. In my
world that's user research, an honest read on your team's
actual capabilities, and competitor analysis — and most
teams skip at least one of the three.

It reads as aphorisms, so the value depends entirely on
what you bring to it. Skimmable in an evening, worth
rereading whenever a project turns political.
