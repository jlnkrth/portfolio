## Notes

Mallaby's history of venture capital lands one statistical
point and never lets go: returns follow a power law. Most
bets lose, and everything gets paid for by one or two
outliers. Once you accept that, the weird behavior of VCs
starts looking rational.

The corollary changed how I read term sheets: a fund's
downside is capped at 1x while the upside is unbounded, so
the only fatal mistake is missing the outlier. Passing on
Airbnb costs more than backing ten duds.

Great chapters on the lineage — Arthur Rock freeing the
Traitorous Eight, Don Valentine betting on markets over
founders, YC industrializing the seed round. The craft got
passed hand to hand like a guild secret.

The SoftBank/WeWork ending is the cautionary tale: capital
as a weapon works right up until the power law reminds you
that valuation isn't value.

Useful reading if you build for VC-backed startups. It
explains why your clients behave the way they do.
