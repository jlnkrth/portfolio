## Notes

The core idea survives the military packaging: everything
that happens on your watch is yours. When a release goes
sideways, the useful question isn't "who broke it" but
"what did I fail to communicate, check, or plan." Saying
that out loud as a lead changes the whole room's posture.

There are no bad teams, only bad leaders. The boat-crew
story — swap the leaders, watch the worst crew start
winning — is the best argument I know against blaming
"the talent" for process problems.

The rest of the toolkit, translated to product teams:

1. Believe in the mission or push back before you commit
2. Keep plans simple enough to survive contact with reality
3. Prioritize and execute — one fire at a time
4. Decentralized command: brief the intent, not the steps

The SEAL war stories run long and the corporate parallels
get repetitive by the back half. Skim those; keep the
framework.
