## Notes

Silver's core point: we don't have an information problem,
we have a prediction problem. More data mostly means more
noise, and confident people mistake the noise for signal.

The fox vs. hedgehog split maps cleanly onto product work.
Hedgehogs have one big idea and force everything through
it; foxes hold many small ideas loosely and keep updating.
Foxes forecast better. So do good tech leads.

Think in probabilities, not verdicts. The Bayesian habit —
state a prior, weigh new evidence, revise — is the whole
book in one move. Saying "70% confident" and meaning it
beats sounding certain.

Weather forecasting is the quiet hero chapter. It improved
for decades because forecasts got scored against reality
every single day. Most teams never close that loop on
their own predictions, which is why they never improve.

Overfitting stuck with me as a design lesson too: a model
that explains the past perfectly usually says the least
about the future.
