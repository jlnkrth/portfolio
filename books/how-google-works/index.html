## Notes

The useful idea is "smart creatives" — people who combine
technical depth, business sense, and creative flair — and
the claim that management's only real job is building a
place where they can thrive at scale.

Culture is set early and then it's set. Schmidt and
Rosenberg argue you write down your values while the team
is small, hire against them ruthlessly, and never delegate
hiring. Committees over hiring managers was the spiciest
take, and I've come around to it.

Ship and iterate over polish and launch. Base plans on
technical insight, not market research. Both sound obvious
until you watch a roadmap get built entirely out of
competitor screenshots.

It's dated in places — the Google described here doesn't
quite exist anymore — but as a snapshot of how a platform
era company thought about talent, it holds up.
