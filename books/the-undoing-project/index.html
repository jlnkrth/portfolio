## Notes

Lewis tells the Kahneman and Tversky story as a love story
between two opposite minds, and it works. Danny the anxious
doubter, Amos the confident knife — the ideas came out of
the friction between them.

The science is familiar if you've read Thinking, Fast and
Slow, but seeing the heuristics born in context hits
different: judgment under uncertainty runs on stories, not
statistics, and experts are barely better than anyone else.

The undoing idea itself stuck with me most. Regret is
driven by how easily we can imagine the alternative — we
grieve the near miss more than the distant one. That
asymmetry quietly shapes a lot of decisions.

Best lesson for teams: their collaboration worked because
neither owned an idea. Every draft was joint, every credit
shared — until it wasn't, and the partnership decayed. Ego
accounting kills great pairs.

Also a reminder that great research needs slack. Tversky's
line about being a little underemployed is one I quote to
clients who schedule every hour.
