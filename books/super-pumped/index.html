## Notes

Isaac's Uber book is really a portrait of Travis Kalanick
— relentless, abrasive, willing to break rules until the
rules pushed back. It's the definitive case study of
growth-at-all-costs culture and the bill coming due.

The Greyball chapters are uncomfortable reading for anyone
who builds "move fast" products. Evading regulators with
software wasn't a bug in the culture; it was the culture,
codified and shipped.

What stuck: the values on Uber's wall — "always be
hustlin'," "toe-stepping" — weren't decoration. People
optimized for exactly what leadership rewarded. Culture is
the incentive structure, not the poster.

The 2017 collapse reads like dominoes: Susan Fowler's blog
post, the Greyball exposé, the Waymo lawsuit, the board
coup. Each one survivable alone. Together, fatal.

Founder-friendly governance made Kalanick nearly
impossible to remove even after all that. Worth
remembering when everyone praises founder control.
