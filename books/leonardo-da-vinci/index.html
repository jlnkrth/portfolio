## Notes

The notebooks are the real subject. Seven thousand pages
of to-do lists like describe the tongue of the woodpecker —
curiosity with zero regard for usefulness. Isaacson's
point: that indiscriminate curiosity is exactly what fed
the art. The engineering made the painting better, and
vice versa.

Leonardo shipped almost nothing. He carried the Mona Lisa
around for sixteen years, still tweaking it. Isaacson
treats this generously; I read it as a warning. Genius
plus no deadline equals a garage full of unfinished
prototypes.

He was a designer before the word existed: obsessive about
observation, prototyping, and how things actually work
under the surface. The anatomy dissections just to paint a
smile correctly — that's user research taken to its
logical extreme.

Best takeaway for anyone in a creative field: skills
compound across disciplines. Leonardo's optics fed his
painting, his stagecraft fed his engineering. Being
T-shaped is fine; he argues for being comb-shaped.
