## Notes

Bogle's argument is almost boring, which is the point: the
market return minus costs is all investors as a group can
earn, so the fund with the lowest costs wins over time.
Everything else is marketing.

The chapters on reversion to the mean killed any interest I
had in picking funds. Yesterday's five-star manager is
statistically most likely to be tomorrow's average one —
you're paying active fees for past luck.

What I actually took away:

1. Costs and taxes compound against you just like returns
   compound for you
2. Time in the market beats timing the market, every time
3. Simplicity is a feature — one broad index fund does the
   job
4. Ignore the noise; the industry profits from your
   activity, not your returns

It's long and repetitive, but as an operating manual for
not screwing up your savings, nothing I've read beats it.
