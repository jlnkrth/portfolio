## Notes

The disciplined pursuit of less. The one-liner that
reorganized my calendar: if it isn't a clear yes, it's a
clear no. A 7-out-of-10 project is a 0 — the 90% rule
sounds brutal until you notice how much mediocre work you
were saying yes to out of politeness.

Trade-offs are real and pretending otherwise is how
agencies burn out. You can't do it all; the question is
never "how can I fit both" but "which problem do I want."
McKeown treats that as liberating rather than limiting,
and he's right.

The bit I actually implemented:

1. Build in buffers — estimate, then add 50%
2. Protect time to think; exploration is a job, not slack
3. Say no gracefully but say it — respect beats popularity
4. Remove obstacles instead of adding effort

Overlaps heavily with The ONE Thing, but this is the
better-written book. Where Keller gives you a question,
McKeown gives you a posture.
