## Notes

The fatal assumption: because you understand the technical
work of a business, you understand the business of that
work. I've watched brilliant developers found agencies and
drown in exactly this. I've been the one drowning, too.

The three-personality split is the framework that stuck:

1. Technician — does the work, hates the paperwork
2. Manager — craves order, builds the systems
3. Entrepreneur — lives in the future, hates the present

Most founders are 70% technician, and the business pays
for it.

Work on your business, not just in it. The test: could
someone else run this from documentation alone? The
franchise-prototype lens sounds corporate, but for a small
studio it translates to playbooks, templates, and processes
that don't live in one person's head. Mine included.

The Sarah-and-her-pie-shop narrative device gets
saccharine. Push through — the operating manual underneath
is worth the cringe.
