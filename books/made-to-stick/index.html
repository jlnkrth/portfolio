## Notes

SUCCES: simple, unexpected, concrete, credible, emotional,
stories. It's a checklist for why some ideas spread and
most die. I keep it taped next to every landing page and
pitch deck I review.

The curse of knowledge is the villain of the book, and
it's my job description. Once you know how the system
works, you physically cannot imagine not knowing it —
which is why engineers write docs nobody understands and
founders pitch features instead of outcomes.

Concrete beats abstract every time. "A man on the moon by
the end of the decade" is sticky; a mission statement
about "leveraging synergies" evaporates on contact. Most
copywriting fixes I make are just swapping abstractions
for something you can picture.

Finding the core means forced prioritization — if you say
three things, you say nothing. Same rule as good interface
design, which is probably why this book aged so well for
me.
