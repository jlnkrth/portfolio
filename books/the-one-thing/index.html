## Notes

The focusing question is the whole book: what's the ONE
thing I can do such that by doing it everything else
becomes easier or unnecessary? I ask a version of it at
the start of every sprint now, and it embarrasses most
of my to-do lists.

The domino metaphor stuck. Line up priorities so knocking
over one small thing sets up the next, bigger one. Most
roadmaps I see are flat lists — no sequencing, no leverage.

What I actually changed after reading it:

1. Time-block the ONE thing first, protect it ruthlessly
2. Stop pretending multitasking works — switching is a tax
3. Break big goals down until "someday" becomes "right now"
4. Treat willpower as a battery and schedule around it

Fair warning: it's one idea stretched to book length. You
could read the first third and stop. The idea is good
enough that I didn't mind.
