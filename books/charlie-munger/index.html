## Notes

Griffin distills Munger into one move: build a latticework
of mental models from many disciplines and run every big
decision through several of them at once. One model is a
hammer, and everything starts looking like a nail.

Circle of competence is the one I use weekly. Knowing the
edge of what you actually understand matters more than how
big the circle is. Most bad calls I've watched happen came
from someone quietly stepping outside theirs.

The models that stuck:

1. Inversion — ask what guarantees failure, avoid that
2. Incentives — never ignore what people are paid to do
3. Margin of safety — leave room to be wrong
4. Social proof — crowds are a signal, not an argument

The real discipline is mostly about not doing things. Sit
on your hands until the odds are heavily in your favor,
then act big. That patience is harder than any analysis.
