## Notes

Rockefeller's edge wasn't vision, it was accounting. He
knew the cost of every barrel, hoop, and rail rebate when
his competitors were guessing. Boring operational rigor,
compounded over decades, beat every flashier rival.

Standard Oil is the original playbook for economies of
scale: own the barrel factories, the pipelines, the tank
cars. Every acquisition made the next one cheaper. It's
the same logic platform companies run today, minus the
antitrust ending.

The ledger habit stuck with me. He recorded every personal
expense from age sixteen in Ledger A and audited himself
like a business. Measurement as self-discipline, a century
before dashboards.

Chernow doesn't sand off the contradictions: ruthless
monopolist, devout Baptist, and the biggest philanthropist
of his era, all the same man. The book refuses to resolve
it, which is why it works.

At 832 pages it's a commitment. Worth it — but read it as
history, not as a how-to.
