## Notes

The premise is quietly radical: growth is a choice, not a
law of nature. Burlingham profiles fourteen companies that
had every chance to scale — Anchor Brewing, Zingerman's,
Clif Bar — and deliberately stayed small to stay great.

The pattern across all of them: ownership stays inside the
company. The moment outside capital shows up, someone else
gets a vote on what success means, and the default vote is
always "bigger."

Mojo is his word for what these companies have — the thing
you feel walking in the door. It comes from intimacy: with
the community, with customers, with employees. Intimacy
doesn't scale, which is exactly the point.

As someone who runs a small studio on purpose, this book
felt like permission. The interesting question isn't "how
big can this get" but "what is this for."
