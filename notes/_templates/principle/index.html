Principle

Delete Half

The best edit is almost always the one that removes something.

When something you made feels off, your first instinct is to add. Another option, another label, another sentence explaining the last one. This is almost always wrong. The fix is usually to delete half and see what breaks.

Nothing breaks, most of the time. That is the uncomfortable part. The things we add feel necessary while we add them, and invisible once they pile up. Deleting is how you find out which was which.

Cut before you polish

Polishing a thing that shouldn't exist is the most expensive kind of work: it looks like progress and produces nothing. So before you tune the spacing on a settings panel, ask whether the panel should exist. Before you rewrite a paragraph, ask whether the essay needs it. Half the time the honest answer is no, and you just saved yourself an afternoon.

A good test: describe the thing out loud without the part you suspect. If the description still makes sense, the part was decoration.

Make deleting cheap

People hoard because deleting feels permanent. It shouldn't be. Keep a graveyard.md at the root of the project and move cut ideas there instead of arguing about them. Nobody has ever gone back for one, but knowing they could makes the cut painless. 

## Graveyard

- Dark mode toggle in onboarding (2026-03) — nobody asked
- Third pricing tier (2026-04) — split the difference, sold neither
- Animated empty states (2026-05) — polish on a screen we want people to leave

Capture cuts in one place so deleting feels reversible.

The file does two jobs. It lowers the cost of cutting, and it becomes a record of your taste — a list of things you were disciplined enough not to ship.

Trust the survivors

What's left after a hard cut is denser than anything you could write or design directly. Each surviving element now does more work, because it has no neighbors to lean on. This is why short essays read as confident and sparse interfaces feel fast: not because less is stylish, but because everything that remains earned its place.

Don't ask what to add. Delete half, and let what survives tell you what the thing is.
