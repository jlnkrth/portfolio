Principle

An LLM Is One Brain Region Pretending to Be a Whole Brain

We’ve been comparing chatbots to brains. Wrong metaphor. A brain is more like a chip: many systems lighting up together. An LLM is closer to one of those regions.

Every few months someone posts a take that LLMs “think like us now.” They point at a clever answer, a chain of reasoning, a model that passes a hard exam. And sure — the text can look like thought. But if you stare at how an actual brain works for five minutes, the comparison falls apart in a useful way.

A brain isn’t one big language engine with a personality. It behaves more like a chip. Different areas activate for different jobs. Vision, motor control, memory, threat detection, language, planning. They talk to each other. They inhibit each other. They hand work off. What we call “thinking” is usually several systems running at once, not one model completing a prompt.

Current LLMs are closer to one of those layers. A strong one. A useful one. Still one layer: they reason and answer from what got pressed into the weights during training, plus whatever you stuff into the context window. That’s powerful. It is not a brain.

Stop comparing chatbots to people

The brain-as-person metaphor sells demos. The brain-as-chip metaphor explains failures. When you treat the model like a whole mind, every miss feels like stupidity. When you treat it like one specialized region, the misses make sense: you asked the language area to also be eyes, hands, long-term memory, and a sense of what just happened in the room.

If you believe… You expect… What you actually get
LLM ≈ whole brain One box that sees, remembers, acts, judges Fluent text that invents missing senses
LLM ≈ one region Great language / pattern completion Same, but you stop asking it to be everything else
Brain ≈ chip with many areas Specialized systems coordinated in time A design brief for agents, tools, and memory

The metaphor isn’t a neuroscience paper. It’s a routing rule for what you ask the model to own alone.

Brains light up in patches

You don’t “run consciousness” as a single forward pass. You look at a face and visual circuits fire. You reach for a cup and motor plans wake up. You smell smoke and older alarm systems jump the queue. Language regions narrate some of that, after or beside it — they don’t replace it.

Even inside one task, the chip is plural. Reading this sentence uses vision, language, working memory, and whatever mood your body brought into the room. The experience feels unified. The machinery is not.

Perception [ eyes, ears, body ]
Memory systems [ short, long, procedural ]
Action [ hands, voice, movement ]
Language / reasoning [ the LLM-shaped part ]
Regulation [ stop, fear, reward, timing ]
What a chat LLM ships alone mostly the language row

Crude map on purpose. The point is plurality, not a Brodmann atlas.

What an LLM actually is

Strip the product marketing and you’re left with a system that predicts useful next tokens given a context. Training compressed a huge pile of text into weights. At runtime it reactivates patterns from that pile, steered by your prompt. It can look like reasoning because language itself carries reasoning shapes. It can look like memory because the training set and the context window hold traces.

What it cannot do, by itself, is what other brain systems do constantly: take a fresh sensory reading of the world, update a body state, practice a motor skill overnight, or decide that this meeting is unsafe and you should leave. Those aren’t “prompt engineering” problems. They’re different substrates.

So when people say the model “knows” something, they usually mean: the pattern was common enough in training, or you put it in the window. That’s one kind of knowing. Your hippocampus doing its weird overnight job is another. Mixing the words makes the product sound smarter than the architecture. 

LLM ALONE
- pattern completion from weights
- short working context you provide
- text in, text out

NOT INCLUDED
- live perception loop
- durable episodic memory
- motor / tool body (unless you attach one)
- multi-system arbitration under time pressure

Tape this next to any “AGI by Christmas” thread. Keeps the claim honest.

The part we keep ignoring

Here’s the actual miss. Brains don’t win because one region is magic. They win because different systems run together. Perception constrains language. Memory constrains planning. Motor feedback corrects the story you were about to tell yourself. Affect changes which plan gets airtime. The intelligence is in the coordination as much as in any single area.

An LLM product that only chats pretends coordination is optional. You type, it answers, you trust the paragraph. There’s no second system that saw the screen, no third system that checked last week’s notes, no fourth system that refused the action because the stakes were wrong. Unless you build those on purpose, you’re running a language region with the volume turned up and calling it a mind.

Left side is loops. Right side is a completion. Different machines.

What to do with that

If you’re building with these models, stop asking one region to fake a nervous system. Give it the other systems as tools and files: scrapers and browsers for perception, wikis and findings.md for memory, APIs and CLIs for action, evals and human review for regulation. That’s not “bolting features on.” That’s admitting the architecture.

And if you’re evaluating the tech, judge it as a strong language-and-pattern region — sometimes shockingly strong — not as a person in a box. The hype shrinks. The usefulness gets clearer. You stop being disappointed that the chip can’t see, and you start wiring eyes.

The next wave of useful AI won’t be “a bigger region.” It’ll be better chips: specialized pieces that activate for different jobs and actually talk to each other. Brains already had that design. We just kept benchmarking the language area and calling it the whole animal.
